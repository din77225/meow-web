1. Inputs

Source video + character sheets + prop sheets + location guide.

Character sheets

Take a screenshot of each character and create a reference sheet in OpenArt → Image → GPT Image 2.5.

Input: A screenshot of the character from your video. A blurry frame is okay.

Character sheet prompt (swap the outfit words for your person)Image 1 is a blurry screenshot from a security camera. Make a clean, photorealistic character reference sheet of this man: three equal panels side by side on a plain mid-grey studio background. Left: head-and-shoulders close-up, facing the camera, calm expression. Middle: full-body front view of his outfit from the neck down. Right: full-body back view, including the back of his head. Keep his build, hair and outfit from the screenshot: an adult man with short dark hair, a grey short-sleeve T-shirt, blue jeans and dark sneakers. The same man in all three panels. Even soft light, thin grey dividers, no text, labels or logos.

Output: A character reference sheet.

Prop sheets

Do the same for any object that matters to the scene, like the pickup.

Input: A screenshot of the object from your video.

Prop sheet prompt (swap the vehicle words for your object)Image 1 is a blurry screenshot from a security camera. Make a clean, photorealistic vehicle reference sheet of this pickup truck: six separate views on a plain mid-grey studio background. Front three-quarter, exact side profile, rear three-quarter, front view, top view with the open cargo bed, and the same front three-quarter with only the driver door open, as in the clip. Keep its real size and proportions: a small light silver-white two-door compact pickup with a short extended cab, small rear side windows, a short open cargo bed with dark lining, dark grey bumpers and trim, and simple silver wheels. Do not turn it into a large four-door truck. The same vehicle in every view. No people, no damage, blank number plate, no brand badges, text or logos. Even soft light, landscape 3:2.

Output: A prop reference sheet.

Location guide

Input: A screenshot showing the location and its main landmarks.

Location guide promptCreate a photorealistic cinematic location guide from this screenshot. Preserve the visible layout, terrain, landmarks and their relative positions. Remove people, animals, vehicles, text and logos. Show the empty setting.

Output: An empty location reference image.

2. Write the prompt

First, watch your clip and write a rough draft in your own words: what happens, and the camera angle, shot type and movement you want for each section. It does not need to sound like a finished prompt.

Paste your notes with the helper below into your AI chatbot to get a complete prompt for OpenArt: preservation, references, camera coverage, then style and audio. You can also attach the clip if your chatbot supports inspecting video. Check the result against your clip.

Not sure which angle to try? Start with a front-facing medium shot, a side-on full-body shot, or a close-up of a character’s reaction. Choose one that shows the action clearly.

3. Car example

@Video1: source video. @Image1–@Image3: character sheets (father sheet above). @Image4: pickup sheet above. @Image5: location guide above.

Car example prompt from the tutorialKeep the people, their positions, movement paths, event order and timing in @Video1. Change the camera coverage and cinematic look, not the action. The father starts off-screen, never inside the pickup. Appearance references: @Image1 is the father, @Image2 the first-exiting boy, @Image3 the second-exiting boy, @Image4 the pickup, and @Image5 the location guide. Keep the boys distinct. Use @Image5 for the yard layout and landmarks. @Video1 controls movement and geography. 0–1s: Front three-quarter wide at headlight height. The pickup is already rolling backward with the boys in the cab; the father is off-screen. 1–4s: Side tracking with the pickup as the first boy gets out and moves alongside it, reaching toward the vehicle. The other boy remains in the cab. 4–5.7s: Continue tracking from the side as the second boy gets out and runs away from the pickup. The first boy remains beside it. 5.7–8s: Ground-level rear-following wide behind the father as he runs toward the pickup. Preserve everyone's source movement paths and the father's urgent shouting. 8–14s: Ground-level side wide at the yard edge, following the pickup as it rolls backward over the edge and falls down the slope. Show the source vehicle trajectory; keep the camera outside that trajectory. This is newly filmed cinematic coverage, not the original elevated security-camera view. 14–17s: Low wide from behind the boys as the father turns toward them, still shaken. Father: "Quem que mexeu?" 17–22s: Medium shot of the father bending toward the boys with his hands on his knees, then reverse-angle on the answering boy. Boy: "Fui eu, pai." 22–26s: Medium three-shot. Father: "Não tem problema. Vem, me dá um abraço." He opens his arms, approaches and embraces the nearer boy, beginning to relax. 26–29s: The father extends his free arm to welcome the other boy, draws him into the embrace and wraps that arm around him. Father: "Não foi nada." Style: natural daylight, restrained blue-green shadows, warm skin and soft highlights. Newly rendered cinematic footage throughout. No on-screen words, subtitles, timestamps, CCTV logos or source-footage inserts. Audio: use @Video1's original Portuguese voices, vocal tone, cries, breathing and environmental sound. During the incident around 5–7s, the father's urgent line is "Sai, sai, sai pro lado!" The quoted Portuguese lines above guide the spoken wording; use "Fui eu, pai" as the child's single answer. Follow the source speech rhythm and pauses within the corresponding action beats, without stretching the action to fit a sentence. Synchronize each visible speaker's mouth precisely to their spoken words. Retain the frightened delivery until the father approaches and embraces the boys. No English translation, extra dialogue, music or narration.

A lesson from this example: The final reassurance is written once in this prompt, while the source repeats it. My result omitted a repetition. Before reusing this structure, verify the wording, speaker and repetitions. If you cannot verify the dialogue, leave out quoted lines and ask the model to follow the source audio instead.

4. Generate and select

In OpenArt, select Seedance 2.5 Draft, 480p (to save credits while testing). Upload the video and all reference sheets, match the labels to the prompt, turn Audio on, keep Auto Polish off, and generate. Upgrade your selected drafts to 1080p later.

Compare the results, keep the good sections and combine them in your editor.

Sound: Keep one continuous audio track across your edit so voices do not jump between takes. You can use a selected generated take or the original audio. If you use the original, check that the generated actions and lip movements still match; replacing the soundtrack does not fix timing.

When a result does not match

The angle barely changed: Choose a clearly different camera position and framing, then test one angle.

The action or timing changed: Remove descriptions that conflict with the clip. Let the video guide the action; simplify the shot plan or test a shorter section.

A face or object changed: Check the reference labels and sheets against the source. References help consistency but do not guarantee it. Keep the usable sections and retry only what you need.
