Week 1

Week 2

Planning, and image craft

Plan the piece properly, learn what actually controls an image, and steer with reference images instead of guessing. Then turn a still into a moving shot.

You leave with: your key frames, and one of them moving
Week 3

Your asset kit, and your first shots

Keep a character the same person across shots, build the locations and props your piece needs, and start generating video from more than words.

You leave with: your asset kit, and your first shots
Week 4

Directing like a director

The video prompt in depth: camera and lighting language, carrying continuity from one shot to the next, and which model to reach for depending on what the shot needs.

You leave with: the rest of your shots
Week 5

Editing, and working with real footage

Assemble everything into one piece, rescue shots instead of paying to regenerate them, and turn real footage into AI video.

You leave with: your piece cut and locked
Week 6

Sound, finish, publish

Music, voice and sound where your piece still needs them, then finishing and exporting it properly, ready for premiere night.

You leave with: a finished video

Questions

Before you decide

I've never made an AI video. Is this too advanced?

No. It's taught from zero and assumes nothing. The only thing I'd ask is that you arrive with a rough idea of what you want to make. You don't need to know how.

I'm not a total beginner. Will it be too basic?

It moves quickly, and the Thursday working sessions are where you can push further on your own project. But if you already produce AI video professionally, this probably isn't for you.

What if I can't make the live times?

Recordings go up after each session and you keep them for 12 months. Sessions are set for North American evenings, so if you're outside those timezones you'd watch the class recording and bring your questions and your work to the Thursday session, or post them in the group and I'll answer there. People in other timezones do the course this way.

Can I make a music video?

Yes, the same skills apply to visuals cut to a song, and I don't teach a separate music video workflow.

⚠ One condition: your finished song must be ready by week one. The whole piece is planned and cut to the track, so without it you can't start. Making the song is not part of this course.

What kind of project should I bring?

Anything with characters, places and shots: a short film, an ad, a trailer, a piece for your own brand. Same skills either way. You decide before we start.

Which tools do I need, and what will they cost?

We work mainly in one platform so nobody gets lost. Free alternatives exist and I'll name them, but I won't pretend they're as good, and you'll spend more of your time fighting them. Budget roughly $50 to $100 USD for a platform subscription over the six weeks; it isn't included in the course price. We did the math with the most expensive model on the board and generous retries, and even that worst case came in under $100 of generation spend for the whole final project. Most people spend well under that. In week one you learn to read the exact cost of any generation before you click, so your spend is never a surprise.

Won't this be out of date in six months?

Some of it, yes. That's the field. Which is why the principles come first and the tools are the demonstration, and why we spend time on how to judge a new model when it appears. Recordings and updates run 12 months.

Will you teach me how to get clients or what to charge?

It isn't the focus of the course, and I'd rather be straight about that. Pricing depends on your situation and finding clients depends on your niche, so there's no single answer I can hand you. It's something we can talk through in the live sessions.
